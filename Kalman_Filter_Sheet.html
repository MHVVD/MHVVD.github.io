The Linear Kalman Filter

The intuition explained plainly, with the mathematics distilled onto a single printable sheet

The Problem

How does the self-driving car know exactly where it is? Physics models aren't perfect. Sensors have static and noise.

The Solution: The Kalman filter is an optimal estimator that loops endlessly, fusing our physics prediction with our noisy sensor readings to find the truth.

NOTE: This is the foundation. It assumes LINEARITY.

Step 1: The Prediction

$$\hat{x}_k = F\hat{x}_{k-1} + Bu_k$$ $$P_k = FP_{k-1}F^T + Q$$
SymbolMeaning
$\hat{x}_{k-1}$Previous state (where was I?)
$P_{k-1}$Previous uncertainty
$\hat{x}_k$Current state prediction
$P_k$Current uncertainty
$F$State Transition Matrix
$u_k$Control input (gas, steering)
$B$Control matrix
$Q$Process noise (wind, bumps)

Step 2: The Measurement

$$z_k = H\hat{x}_k + v_k$$
SymbolMeaning
$z_k$Sensor reading (GPS, LIDAR)
$H$Observation Matrix
$v_k$Sensor noise
$R$Measurement Covariance

The Magic: The Kalman Gain

$$K = P_k H^T \left(H P_k H^T + R\right)^{-1}$$

Don't let the matrix scare you. Conceptually, it asks: Who do I trust more?

$$K \approx \frac{\text{Prediction uncertainty}}{\text{Prediction uncertainty} + \text{Sensor uncertainty}}$$

The Decision Logic:

  • If R is large, then K → 0. Ignore the sensor and trust our physics prediction.
  • If Pk is large, then K → 1. Ignore physics and trust the sensor.

The Final Update

$$\hat{x}_k = \hat{x}_k^- + K\left(z_k - H\hat{x}_k^-\right)$$ $$P_k = \left(I - KH\right)P_k^-$$
